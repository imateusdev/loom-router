# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Argue about code, not about the person writing it.

## What's expected

This project is small and technical, and most friction here comes from the
same few places. So, concretely:

- **Critique the change, not the contributor.** "This drops the error and the
  failure becomes invisible" is useful. "This is careless work" is not, and
  says nothing the first sentence didn't.
- **Assume the other person had a reason.** Most odd-looking code in this repo
  exists because a provider does something odd. Ask what forced it before
  concluding it's wrong.
- **Nobody here is owed your time, and you aren't owed theirs.** This is
  maintained by one person alongside other work. A PR may wait. An issue may
  be closed as out of scope. Neither is an insult, and neither is an
  invitation to escalate.
- **English is a second language for most people in open source, including
  the maintainer.** Read past awkward phrasing rather than reacting to it.

## What isn't acceptable

Harassment, insults, and personal attacks. Discriminatory language or jokes,
including about nationality, gender, sexuality, disability, religion, or
appearance. Sexualized language or imagery. Publishing anyone's private
information without their explicit permission. Sustained disruption of
discussions or deliberate derailing of issues. Repeated contact after someone
has asked you to stop.

This applies in issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, code
comments, and anywhere else this project is represented.

## Enforcement

The maintainer decides what happens, in proportion to what occurred, and is
not obligated to litigate it in public. Typically:

1. A private note explaining what was out of line.
2. A public correction in the thread, and the thread locked.
3. A temporary block from interacting with the repository.
4. A permanent ban.

Serious incidents can start at any step, including the last one.
